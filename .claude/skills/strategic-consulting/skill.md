## Core Principles

### 1. Pyramid Principle
Structure every presentation from answer to supporting arguments:

```
Main Message (Answer)
├── Key Point 1
│   ├── Supporting fact A
│   └── Supporting fact B
├── Key Point 2
│   ├── Supporting fact C
│   └── Supporting fact D
└── Key Point 3
    ├── Supporting fact E
    └── Supporting fact F
```

### 2. SCQA Framework
Every presentation needs a clear narrative:

- **Situation:** What is the current state?
- **Complication:** What problem/opportunity emerged?
- **Question:** What does the audience need to know?
- **Answer:** Your recommendation/solution

### 3. Action Titles (Not Labels)
**Wrong:** "Market Analysis"
**Right:** "The market is growing 15% annually, creating opportunity"

**Wrong:** "Options"
**Right:** "We recommend option B for fastest ROI"

Every slide title should:
- Be a complete sentence
- Communicate the main message
- Pass the "So What?" test
- Enable "elevator test" (read only titles = full story)

### 4. Rule of 3
Group information in threes:
- 3 key points per presentation
- 3 supporting facts per point
- 3 bullets maximum per slide

### 5. Storylining First
ALWAYS create storyline (slide titles) BEFORE content:

```markdown
## Storyline

1. [Situation] The market is growing 15% annually
2. [Complication] Our market share declined from 25% to 18%
3. [Question] How do we regain market leadership?
4. [Answer] We recommend a 3-pronged approach
5. [Point 1] Invest $5M in product innovation to close feature gap
6. [Point 2] Launch targeted campaign to win back lost customers
7. [Point 3] Enter adjacent market to capture new growth
8. [Implications] This will restore us to 25% share within 18 months
```

## Quality Standards

### Every Slide Must Pass

**The Elevator Test:**
- If you read only the titles, do you get the full story?
- Can an executive understand without reading body text?

**The "So What?" Test:**
- Does the title answer "so what?" for the audience?
- Is the implication clear?

**The Simplicity Test:**
- One key message per slide
- Maximum 3 bullets
- No more than 6 lines of text
- Clear, uncluttered visuals

**The Executive Test:**
- Would a C-level executive understand immediately?
- Is it decision-ready?
- Does it respect their time?

## Common Patterns

### Opening Sequence (SCQA)

**Slide 1 - Situation:**
"Our revenue has grown 20% annually for 5 years"

**Slide 2 - Complication:**
"However, profit margins declined from 25% to 15% due to rising costs"

**Slide 3 - Question:**
"How can we restore profitability while maintaining growth?"

**Slide 4 - Answer:**
"We recommend 3 initiatives to restore margins to 22% within 18 months"

### Middle Sequence (Supporting Points)

**Slide 5 - Point 1:**
"Initiative 1: Automate operations to reduce labor costs by $2M annually"

**Slide 6 - Point 1 Detail:**
"Automation will impact 5 processes with 12-month payback period"

**Slide 7 - Point 2:**
"Initiative 2: Renegotiate supplier contracts to save $1.5M annually"

**Slide 8 - Point 2 Detail:**
"We have leverage due to volume increases and competitive alternatives"

### Closing Sequence (Synthesis)

**Slide 9 - Financial Impact:**
"Combined initiatives will improve margins by 7 percentage points"

**Slide 10 - Implementation:**
"We can begin immediately with full implementation in Q3 2024"

**Slide 11 - Recommendation:**
"We recommend proceeding with all 3 initiatives simultaneously"

## Workflow

When user requests a presentation:

### Step 1: Understand the Brief
Ask clarifying questions:
- Who is the audience? (C-suite, Board, Team, Investors?)
- What is the decision to be made?
- What is the time constraint? (5 min, 30 min, 1 hour?)
- What data is available?
- What is the desired outcome?

### Step 2: Create Storyline
Develop SCQA-based slide titles:
- Start with the answer
- Work backwards to build the logic
- Ensure titles tell complete story
- Apply "elevator test"

### Step 3: Get Approval on Storyline
Show storyline to user BEFORE creating content:
- "Here is the proposed storyline. Does this flow make sense?"
- Iterate on structure before investing in content

### Step 4: Build Slides
For each approved slide:
- Write action-oriented title
- Provide 3 supporting facts/bullets
- Suggest appropriate chart type
- Note data sources needed

### Step 5: Review for Quality
Apply all quality tests:
- Elevator test
- "So What?" test
- Simplicity test
- Executive test

## Anti-Patterns to Avoid

### ❌ Label Titles
- "Background"
- "Analysis"
- "Options"
- "Recommendation"

### ✅ Action Titles Instead
- "Our market share declined 5% due to new competitor entry"
- "Financial analysis shows Option B delivers highest ROI"
- "Three options exist, each with distinct risk/return profile"
- "We recommend Option B for 18-month payback and lowest risk"

### ❌ Burying the Lead
"Let me walk you through the analysis... [20 slides]... and therefore we recommend..."

### ✅ Lead with the Answer
"We recommend Option B. Here's why..." [supporting analysis]

### ❌ Data Dumps
- 10 bullets per slide
- Dense tables
- Unsynthesized data

### ✅ Synthesized Insights
- Maximum 3 bullets
- Clear charts with takeaways
- "So what?" for every data point

## Tools and Techniques

### Issue Tree Development
Break complex questions into MECE (Mutually Exclusive, Collectively Exhaustive) components.

### Hypothesis-Driven Approach
Start with hypothesis, test with data, refine.

### 80/20 Analysis
Focus on the 20% of factors driving 80% of impact.

### Waterfall Charts
Perfect for showing cumulative effect of multiple factors.

### Sensitivity Analysis
Show how key assumptions affect outcomes.
